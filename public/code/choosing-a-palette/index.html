Choosing a Palette

When it comes to choosing a palette for a project, there are too many options. A lot of these options and choices are naturally narrowed down by the both the core of the project and its scope; pixel art, for example, allows for the use of a breadth of colours — various shades, highlights, and supporting hues — to set the tone or convey a particular feeling or mood. When it comes to designing websites, however, there’s rarely any need (other than for an a bit of artistic flair, perhaps) to have more than a few base colours.

On paper, it should be markedly easier to choose a palette for a website, but even those aforementioned base colours can be elusive. I’ve never been a big fan of neutral gradients such as the following:

#000000
#0d0d0d
#1a1a1a
#b3b3b3
#f2f2f2

It’s too neutral. It’s boring. Add in a singular splash of colour — a kind of signature, something that catches the eye — and you have an almost identical design to every major startup or company out there: Facebook blue. Spotify green. Netflix red. It isn’t necessary for me to even display the colours; they’re easy enough to picture from the words alone.

The fact remains that websites, unlike pixel art, simply do not need that many colours, but that didn’t stop me from seeking inspiration from those more charming and intentional palettes, even if the sheer amount of choice had me scratching my head.

Eventually, I settled on one particular palette which, at the time of writing, forms the basis of this website’s design:

I love the choice of colours here; I love the contrast, the muted hues, and the overall warmth that it brings even to the traditionally colder shades. It’s one of the more pleasing palettes I’ve seen.

But do I really need all of those colours? Do I need to incorporate all of them? What if I want something a little bit darker, for the sake of contrast? A small amount of tweaking — mostly just by shifting the brightness up or down — and trimming of unnecessary colours led to the following (and currently final) palette:

#121118
#8e9593
#7c7b77
#cf982e

The first two colours — background and text, respectively — are high contrast for the sake of readability, whereas the grey is for the more muted text that appears in the little boxes that I use for tangents. The yellow, applied to links and the home page’s title, acts as a singular ‘pop’ of colour to prevent things from being boring and stale. If I need other colours for emphasis, link styles, or artistic flair, I have the rest of the palette to draw upon, which is exactly what I did to make both the little palette bar on the home page and the site’s favicon.

I adore the range of colours in Phenomenician’s palette, and it was an easy choice to make when I was typing up the CSS. There were other options, too, of course, but it’s unlikely that I’ll be changing the design of this site any time soon; nonetheless, I’ll list a few of my favourite palettes below, along with the resources by which I found them.

RESOURCES

Coolors

An all-in-one palette generation tool. Primarily lists colours in HEX, but also converts to RGB, HSL, CMYK, and others. Provides a wealth of information for any chosen colour — complimentary shades, ramps, and contrast — and also features an image picker tool and trending palettes.

Lospec

A pixel art focused website featuring a wide range of user made palettes, artwork, tutorials, and more. This is where I draw most of my inspiration from.
